*By Saurabh Mukherjea*

Saurabh Mukherjea studies a small group of Indian listed companies that compounded shareholder wealth steadily for well over a decade, and asks what they have in common.

## Key ideas

- **Consistent compounders.** The screen is simple and strict: companies that kept growing revenue and earning high returns on capital year after year, through good and bad cycles.
- **Sustainable moats, not one-off wins.** Using John Kay's lens of architecture, reputation, innovation and brands, the book shows how durable advantages are built slowly.
- **Capital allocation is the real job.** The best firms reinvest at high returns, avoid ego-driven diversification and stay disciplined on leverage.
- **Boring is beautiful.** Predictable, well-governed businesses in unglamorous categories often beat exciting stories.
- **Patience as an edge.** Buying quality and holding it for years beats trying to time the market.

## Takeaway

Look for businesses with a long record of high returns, honest management and disciplined reinvestment, then give them time.
