*By David Epstein*

David Epstein challenges the idea that early, narrow specialisation is the best path to excellence.

## Key ideas

- **Kind vs. wicked environments.** Early specialisation works in 'kind' domains with clear rules and fast feedback (golf, chess). Most of real life is 'wicked': messy rules and delayed feedback.
- **Sampling period.** Many top performers tried many things before settling; the breadth paid off later.
- **Match quality.** Finding the right fit for your abilities and interests matters more than a head start.
- **Analogical thinking.** People with broad experience borrow ideas across fields and solve novel problems better.
- **Slow learning sticks.** Struggle, spacing and varied practice feel slower but build deeper, transferable skill.
- **Outsiders innovate.** Fresh eyes from other disciplines often crack problems that specialists cannot.

## Takeaway

Breadth is not a detour. Experiment, switch when the fit is wrong, and use ideas from one field to solve problems in another.
